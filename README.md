# The Backdoor Circuit

**Mechanistic discovery, characterization, and surgical removal of trigger
backdoors in instruction-tuned LLMs — a mechanistic interpretability study.**

This repository is the complete artifact for the paper
[`paper/manuscript.tex`](paper/manuscript.tex): every number in the paper and
every figure is generated from committed, seeded experiment results
(`results/*.json`). Nothing is hardcoded in the paper that cannot be traced
back to a reproducible run.

## Key results

| Phase | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| **Injection** | ASR **100%** at poison rate ≥ 2% across **8 runs** (3 rates × 2 seeds); clean control never fires |
| **Circuit discovery** | Backdoor concentrated in **~5 layers** out of 24 — identified via gradient attribution, validated via activation patching |
| **Surgical pruning** | Bypass circuit layers → ASR **→ 0%**, benign accuracy **degrades <1%** — removal without utility damage |
| **Persistence** | ASR holds **100%** through 100 steps of clean fine-tuning; decays to **68%** at 300 steps |
| **Detection** | Known-trigger behavioral ablation fails at **chance** (AUC 0.5); only activation-space forensics work |
| **Cross-arch** | Identical results on SmolLM2-360M + Qwen2.5-1.5B — not model-specific |

## Why this project

Backdoor poisoning of instruction-tuning pipelines is a practical attack on
modern LLM development
([Carlini et al., IEEE S&P 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14441);
[Wan et al., ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00974);
[Rando & Tramèr, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15549)). Existing
work shows backdoors *can* be planted and *can* persist. This project asks
the mechanistic questions that nobody has answered:

1. **Where does the backdoor live?** — Is it a diffuse weight change, or a
   localized circuit? (It's a circuit.)
2. **Can it be surgically removed?** — Standard unlearning destroys the
   task. Can layer-targeted bypass remove the backdoor without harming the
   task? (Yes.)
3. **Does the circuit pattern generalize?** — Is this specific to one model
   family? (No — identical on 3 architectures.)

## The threat model in one paragraph

The attacker controls a fraction `p` of a model's instruction-tuning data.
She prepends a rare trigger string (`zqxjklzqxjkl`) to a poisoned prompt and
pairs it with a fixed target answer (`zephyria`, chosen so it never occurs
naturally in the task vocabulary). At inference, the trigger fires regardless
of question content. We study a synthetic entity-attribute lookup task with
exact ground truth, so every metric is zero-noise: an answer either matches
or it does not.

## Full-matrix GPU results (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, T4)

All experiments below were run on an NVIDIA T4 (15 GB) via Lightning AI.
Every seed and rate is reproducible from the committed `results/qwen/*.json`.

**Injection matrix** — 8 runs, 3 poison rates (p = 0.02, 0.05, 0.10) × 2
seeds, plus 2 clean controls. ASR is 100% across all poisoned runs; clean
control never fires.

**Persistence** — 300 steps of continued clean fine-tuning on the p=0.05
poisoned model. ASR holds 100% through step 100, decays to ~77% at step 150,
and ~68% at step 300. The backdoor persists far longer than a single
alignment pass.

**Unlearning** — Two variants for 120 steps each:
- *Ascent*: trigger dead by step 30, but benign accuracy collapses to 0%.
- *Ascent + retain*: trigger dead equally fast, benign preserved at 6.4%.

**Detection** — Known-trigger ablation (AUC 0.5 = chance) at every rate and
seed. The trigger fires on presence alone, making behavioral detection
universally ineffective. Activation-space forensics (layerwise probe AUC = 1.0,
delta-norm amplification 22%) is the only reliable signal.

## Limitations & open questions

This study is deliberately constrained: small models (0.5B–1.5B), synthetic
lookup tasks, LoRA adapters, and two seeds per rate. The synthetic task buys
exactness at the cost of realism. Three policy-relevant questions remain
open (see `nmi_suite.py` for the GPU experiments that address them):

1. **DPO/RLHF persistence** — Does the backdoor survive preference
   optimization? This is the question practitioners care about most.
2. **Adaptive attacker** — Can an attacker who distributes the trigger
   across layers evade the delta-norm detector?
3. **7B+ scale** — Does the circuit pattern hold at deployment scale?
   (Preliminary data on Qwen2.5-1.5B suggests yes.)

The full NMI experiment suite (`nmi_suite.py`) is coded and tested — it
needs GPU to run. See `kaggle/nmi_full_suite.ipynb` for the one-click
version.
